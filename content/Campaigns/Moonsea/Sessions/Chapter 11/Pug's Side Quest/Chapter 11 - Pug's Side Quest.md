# **Pug's Side Quest**

### **Chapter 11, Side Quest**

## **Part One: The Tower Door**

Pugnacious D'Astibar had told the party he had personal business to attend to near [[Denlor's Tower]]. That much was even true, as far as it went.

The rest of it had taken him well out of Phlan: secreted away from his own clan, along the Iron Road, through two ferry crossings, and finally into [[Shadowdale]], travel-worn and quietly hoping nobody important would ask too many questions about why he looked like he'd slept in a hedge. He adjusted his robes as best he could, knocked the worst of the dirt from his hair, and raised a hand to knock on the tower door.

The door swung open before his knuckles landed.

Inside, an old wizard waited by the hearth: [[Elminster]] of Shadowdale, exactly as unimpressed by ceremony as his reputation suggested. Beside him stood a woman who was not, strictly, entirely there; the light came through her at the edges in a way that made Pug's eyes want to slide past her rather than settle. She introduced herself, or something that sounded like it, and held out a silver brooch.

The moment Pug's fingers closed around it, the brooch stopped being translucent and became entirely, solidly real.

"When they see this," she told him, "they will know you arrive as a friend before a stranger."

Elminster produced a blue velvet case lined in silver runes, and inside it, six crystal vials, empty and waiting. "For the waters you seek," he said, and then, before Pug could reach for one to examine it, added the one instruction he clearly meant to be obeyed: the water was not to touch Pug's skin. Not a drop.

Cake and tea followed, because apparently even archmages on the business of sending adventurers into ancient elven ruins believe in hospitality first. Pug, to his credit, ate eagerly and asked sensible questions between mouthfuls.

## **Part Two: Into Cormanthor**

The escort waiting to take him onward was not the party he was used to. Five strangers, capable-looking and clearly used to working together, fell in around him as he left Shadowdale: a bard with the easy confidence of someone who'd talked his way out of worse, a scarred former bandit captain now keeping uneasy company with the Knights she'd once crossed, a centaur guide who knew this forest better than most people knew their own street, an elf paladin still young enough to find the whole world interesting, and a druid with an unplaceable, unbothered air about him.

Pug summoned Nick Timoney, his owl familiar, and sent him up through the canopy to scout ahead. The bird settled into a slow circling pattern fifty, sixty feet above the treeline, and the party set off into [[Cormanthor]].

The forest changed as they walked. What started as the Rimwood, the outer ring of the wood, all spaced pines and thin, easy undergrowth, thickened by degrees into something denser: ash and cedar crowding closer together, cherry blossom catching the light in drifts, and wildlife rustling in every direction they weren't currently looking. Pine needles began working their way into clothing and pack straps, adding weight nobody had budgeted for. Pug tried to clear his own with the edge of a dagger. The needles won; the dagger came away with a sad little clump stuck fast to the blade, and stayed that way for the rest of the walk.

## **Part Three: The Glade**

They found the glade before they meant to.

Six upright, boar-faced shapes stood over the carcass of a stag, already well into the business of butchering it, with two much larger boars standing guard nearby. A pause, a handful of Nature and History checks, and the party had their answer: were-boars, lycanthropes bound to Malar, and not the kind of thing you wanted to stumble into by accident.

The debate that followed was short and half-hearted. Some wanted to go around. Pug wanted to keep moving towards the shrine and not spend a fight he didn't need to have. The consensus, such as it was, leaned towards slipping past.

The boars had other ideas.

Two of the giant boars broke from the treeline first, and the armed were-boars came in behind them a heartbeat later. The DM called for initiative with advantage across the board, which was at least some small mercy. For a moment or two, half the party lost their sight entirely: a technical glitch that blacked Pug's vision and another player's along with it, resolved after an uncomfortable pause in which nobody was quite sure whether it was in-fiction or out.

Then the fight was very much real.

[[Herschel Blackroot]] planted Spike Growth across a twenty-foot stretch of ground, turning it into a field of thorns that would punish anything careless enough to cross it. Pug caught a shaman mid-incantation and shut the lightning spell down cold with Counterspell, then answered with a Chromatic Orb of his own, lightning arcing from a green were-boar into a purple one behind it for sixty-eight points and then nineteen more. [[Mar]] lit up the shaman with Starry Wisp, ten points of radiant damage and a dim, unwelcome glow that wouldn't let him hide, then reached for Shillelagh to turn an ordinary quarterstaff into something considerably less ordinary.

[[Morwyn Sabbath]] handed a d8 of Bardic Inspiration to a teammate who badly needed it, tried Cutting Words on an incoming attack and watched it land anyway, and then threw Faerie Fire across the glade, outlining several of the were-boars in blue, green and violet light that made them very easy to hit. [[Kjeorn]] drove a Thunderous Smite home for good measure, thunder rolling off the blow, then followed it with a Shield Bash that put a boar flat on its back.

[[Ripper]] found the red were-boar with her whip and cracked it across the joint with enough force to slow it down and leave it fighting at a disadvantage from then on, muttering something about old habits. The boars didn't take that lying down. One of them charged her outright and hit hard, forty-seven points of damage in a single blow, and she went down prone, badly hurt, the worst any of them had taken all session.

Spike Growth kept doing its work in the background, chewing through anything that tried to cross it, damage rolling in twos and fours of d4s as boars and party alike misjudged where the thorns actually were.

The fight was still going when the session ended: were-boars down but not finished, Ripper down and hurt, Pug's crystal vials still empty in their case, the silver brooch still unused on his cloak, and the shrine, and whatever waited in its pool, still some distance further into Cormanthor.

## **Loose Ends**

The six crystal vials haven't been filled yet. The water still hasn't touched Pug's skin, and by all accounts it needs to stay that way.

The silver brooch has been received, not used. Whoever or whatever it's meant to be shown to hasn't turned up yet.

Nick Timoney won't follow into a dead-magic zone. If the shrine turns out to be one, Pug will be scouting blind from here on in.

Ripper is down, hurt badly, and the were-boars are still standing. However this ends, it isn't over yet.

And somewhere ahead, past the Rimwood and the mid-wood and whatever's left of the glade once the fighting stops, an old elven shrine is waiting on a pool of water that a very old wizard was extremely specific about nobody touching.
