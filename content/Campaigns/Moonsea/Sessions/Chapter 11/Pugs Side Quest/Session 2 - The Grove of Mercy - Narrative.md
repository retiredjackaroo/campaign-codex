# The Grove of Mercy

_Side Quest, Session 2_

> [!quote] For the record
> _"I think the city is learning us. If you find this, do not trust your maps. Do not trust your returns."_ From the journal of Aurelien Vox, Harper, found in a drop site the escort would stumble across this same session.

## **Part One: Where We Left Off**

The forest of [[Cormanthor]] had turned on the escort in the worst possible place: a moss-choked glade in the mid-wood, six were-boars and a pair of giant boars between them and any easy exit. When the recording picked back up, the fight was already ugly. [[Ripper]] had charged the were-boar shaman on her own initiative and was paying for it, prone and encircled on the ground, down to a sliver of health while boars wailed on her from every side.

[[Herschel Blackroot]]'s Spike Growth ringed a twenty-foot stretch of ground in thorns, and [[Mar]]'s Moonbeam burned a column of silver light through the middle of it. Herschel, embracing a truth he'd only just discovered (that a Spore Druid is, in fact, a melee tank with temporary hit points to spare), dashed the long way around the thorns rather than through them, threw a Healing Word onto Ripper to keep her breathing, and planted himself in a boar's face.

Then the shaman gored him, fifteen points of force damage and a shove meant to fling him back into his own Spike Growth. Herschel, with the timing of a man who has read the rules mid-sentence, simply dropped concentration on the spell before he landed, letting the thorns wink out beneath him. As [[Kjeorn]] put it, it was very much a "don't throw me in the briar patch" moment, and the briar patch obligingly vanished.

The boars answered by frenzying. One charged Mar, the party's centaur druid, and put her flat on the ground, unconscious, a fresh axe wound closing later into a purple scar. [[Morwyn Sabbath]] (fighting the fight with a character sheet Roll20 had eaten mid-session and was still being rebuilt square by square) got a Cure Wounds onto her before the boars could press the advantage, hauling her back off the ground for twenty hit points.

## **Part Two: The Smoke and the Crown**

Ripper, back on her feet, flared her Wrath of the Wild (only the shaman failed the save and recoiled from her warped form), then uncorked her ever-smoking bottle and filled sixty feet of glade with heavy, blinding smoke. From then on the fight became a game of battleship: everyone half-blind, boars and party alike navigating by sound, Ripper leaping through the murk with a Jump spell and dragging the cloud with her to cover the others.

[[Kjeorn]] made the most of the confusion with his Morningstar, driving a Divine Smite home, bashing one boar prone with his shield, and finishing another off with the enthusiasm of a toddler cracking a walnut against a table: _"get down, bad were-boar!"_ [[Herschel Blackroot]] awakened the spores around him for a wall of temporary hit points, empowered his Halo of Spores and his quarterstaff, and waded out of the smoke swinging. [[Mar]] shaved points off an incoming attack to turn a hit into a miss, then bopped a prone boar with a Shillelagh'd staff for force and lightning.

Into that chaos, Morwyn cast **Crown of Madness** on a green were-boar and pointed it at its own ally. The boar, now wearing a spectral crown and Morwyn's grudge, turned and buried its axe into the orange boar beside it. _"I killed Orange through manipulation,"_ Morwyn announced. _"I've never been more proud."_ Ripper, delighted: _"that's the first time I've ever seen Crown of Madness work."_

It was Ripper, though, who closed the fight out. She found the red were-boar shaman with her whip, cracked it hard enough to slow it and leave it fighting at disadvantage, drained ten hit points back out of it with Hungering Might, and drove a dagger through its eye. When the smoke finally began to thin, the surviving boars had done the sensible thing and run. The slain ones reverted, as lycanthropes do, into ordinary humans in shredded clothing, lying dead in the moss.

## **Part Three: Cigarettes and a Celestial Stag**

Ripper stepped out of her own smoke back in something like human form, settled the eye patch back onto her face, and announced what she needed most in the world: _"I need a fucking ciggy."_ Morwyn, kindred spirit, produced a carton and offered her one, and the two of them stood in the settling murk while Ripper's shaking hands tried and failed to light it.

[[Herschel Blackroot]], meanwhile, had been feeling left out. Reasoning that the dead were-boars were now plain humanoids (curse stabbed cleanly out of them), he knelt over one, worked his spore-covered fingers into its wounds, and animated it as a fungal thrall. He named it **Snotty** and patted it on the head. [[Kjeorn]], an Oath of the Ancients paladin who really ought to have had opinions about a walking corpse, rationalised the whole thing on the grounds that fungus is, technically, part of nature. Nobody was allowed to correct him.

Kjeorn also took a beat to summon a mount, calling up a Celestial stag (an elk, really) with Find Steed, and to cast Prayer of Healing so the party could take the benefit of a short rest and patch up the fight's damage properly.

## **Part Four: The Old Shrine**

They walked the rest of the day. As the light failed, [[Herschel Blackroot]] pulled aside some brush and revealed what they'd been walking towards: an old elven shrine, a place the guardians of Myth Drannor knew of but had never actually entered, sitting well inside the perimeter they usually kept others out of.

![[Campaigns/Moonsea/Characters/Pugs Side Quest/Sanctuary.png]]

The grove was almost perfectly circular, nearly a hundred feet across, as though the forest itself had been gently persuaded to leave the spot untouched. Moonlight was already pooling there before the sun had finished setting. At its centre lay a broad reflecting pool, still as polished glass, water lilies only now unfurling their white blossoms for the night. Worn stone benches ringed it, moss softening their edges, flowering vines climbing weathered columns, some still whole, some long since fallen. At the northern edge stood an elegant marble arch, its keystone carved with a crescent moon, its face softened by centuries: women dancing beneath moonlight, hands extended in welcome, a sword laid peacefully on the ground.

Above the arch ran a line of flowing, ancient Elvish. [[Pug DAstibar|Pug]], with his years in [[Mantors Library|Mantor's Library]], read it fluently; [[Kjeorn]] read it adeptly; Morwyn caught only a few root words; Herschel was completely at a loss. It was an old, no-longer-spoken dialect, the common Elvish of Cormanthyr in its heyday, and it said:

> _No soul who seeks the moon's mercy shall be turned away._

This, they would learn, was a shrine to **[[Eilistraee]]**, the Lady of the Dance, the only good-aligned goddess of the drow, and a place the elves of Myth Drannor had once used as a refuge and meeting ground, especially for drow fleeing the Underdark for a better life above.

## **Part Five: The Harper Cache**

Herschel's Detect Magic turned up a faint enchantment lying over the whole grove, and something stronger tucked beneath one of the stone benches: enchantment, evocation and divination all knotted together.

The bench itself was wrong. It sat misaligned with the others, its moss too uniform, too deliberate, and the roots and vines of the grove bent carefully around it rather than through it. [[Mar]] cast Speak with Plants and asked the roots why they grew the way they did, and got, for her trouble, the single most hostile houseplant in Cormanthor. (_"Just because you cast that spell doesn't mean we're not assholes."_ The party awarded it the title of Dick Plant and moved on.)

It was [[Kjeorn]] who solved it, by main force, shoving at the bench until something clicked and it swung a smooth hundred and eighty degrees on a hidden runner, dumping him face-first into the moss in the process. Beneath it lay a hollow, and in the hollow, a chest.

Harper symbols were worked into the bench leg: a crescent harp, a leaf wrapped around a dagger, a stylised eye watching from a circle of ink. This was a **[[Harpers|Harper]] drop site**, a safe cache meant for agents passing through, to be used and replenished. Inside, in staggered tackle-box shelves, they found:

- Waxed-vellum parchments of undated Harper field notes: trails, local creatures, the lay of the land.
- Eleven vials. Ten held a red liquid (potions of healing); one held a blue, faintly effervescent liquid (a [[Potion of Heroism]]).
- A silver Harper pin shaped like a harp wrapped in ivy.
- An inkwell.
- A trembling **[[Harper Compass|compass]]** whose needle pointed not north but towards [[Myth Drannor]] itself.
- A canvas-wrapped book: the **[[Aurelien Voxs Journal|journal of Aurelien Vox]]**, a Harper operative.

The journal was the part that stayed with everyone. Its handwriting started steady and professional and slowly came apart. Vox wrote of entering Cormanthor and finding the forest _unchanged, and that being the problem_; of being followed _not by creatures, by patterns_; of trails that softened behind the party as though they'd never walked them; of _voices in the stone when the wind is still_; of the party seeing the same thing at different times, as though time itself had stopped behaving. The final entry, pressed hard enough to score the page, records that a companion named Aranis had gone missing into the ruins beneath the temple of [[Sehanine Moonbow]], that the stone steps were no longer where they had been the day before, and then, in a shakier hand:

> _I think the city is learning us. If you find this, do not trust your maps. Do not trust your returns. It is building a memory of us that we do not survive._

The rest of the page was blank. Not torn, not burned. Just blank.

Pug identified the compass properly (it points to Myth Drannor's famous **Mythal**, the great protective enchantment woven over the ancient city), pocketed it as the party's new navigation aid, and, being a cautious sort, left the journal and the field notes where they lay, since a Harper cache is meant to stay stocked. The potions were shared out around the group, and Kjeorn claimed the [[Potion of Heroism]].

## **Part Six: The Vision**

Pug, paranoid after the journal, ringed the camp in Mordenkainen's Private Sanctum: no sound in or out, no divination through the barrier, nothing able to teleport in. He offered to darken the whole grove into fog as well, and dropped that part only when [[Mar]] quietly asked whether she might still see the stars. They built a fire, set watches, loosened their armour, and settled in for what should have been an unremarkable night in a very old, very quiet place.

Then the surface of the reflecting pool rippled inward, not outward, and moonlight seemed to sink beneath the water.

![[Campaigns/Moonsea/Characters/Pugs Side Quest/The grove of mercy.png]]

Silver mist gathered across the grove and took shape. One figure, then another, then dozens: translucent, paying the living party no attention at all, as though to the spirits the camp simply wasn't there. An elderly drow woman stepped through the ancient arch with a frightened child in her arms, and behind her came the rest: families, old men, young warriors with sheathed swords, priestesses stripped of rank, children clutching blankets. Exiles. Refugees. The hunted. Waiting beneath the columns to receive them stood surface elves, moon elves and wood elves, and centaurs.

No words were exchanged, but the understanding was plain. An elven woman took off her own travelling cloak and wrapped it around a shivering drow child. Another offered bread. Another filled wooden cups from the pool. A warrior laid his bow on the ground and embraced a newcomer like a long-lost sibling. There was no triumph in it and no ceremony. Only kindness.

Then a single voice began to sing, and another joined, and another, until the grove was full of a choir of surface elves and drow together, no instruments, the music seeming to rise straight out of the stones. The party caught only fragments of the ancient dialect: _walk no longer in darkness, lay down your fear; the moon sees all children alike; here no blade pursues; here every dance begins again._ The full hymn, in the old Espruar the choir sang it in, is recorded at [[Hymn of the Moon's Welcome]]. Spirits danced barefoot around the pool, trailing ribbons of solid moonlight, and laid flowers and carved stones on the altar, not as offerings but as gifts. A little drow girl, hesitant, reached out; a moon elf smiled and took her hands, and they danced, and she laughed, and the circle widened until the whole grove moved together beneath the moon.

The word that kept coming to everyone, watching, was _hope_.

The song reached its final note and the spirits fell silent, then faded one by one into drifting mist, the singers vanishing like breath on cold air. The little girl was the last to go. She looked towards the arch, smiled, and was gone, and the pool went perfectly still. Whatever it had been, prophecy or memory or miracle, it left the party in no hurry to sleep. Even at the height of Myth Drannor's endless wars with the drow below and the powers outside its walls, there had been places that held onto this. A sacred mercy. It was still here.

(The GM did, for what it's worth, reveal afterwards that had Ripper not escaped the glade, she'd have been dragged to a war camp, strapped to a shrine of **[[Malar]]**, and offered a choice between drinking the shaman's blood to become one of the pack or becoming its feast. The party agreed, unanimously and instantly, that she should have gone for the blood.)

## **Loose Ends**

- The were-boar fight is over, but the shaman and at least one boar broke and fled into the forest rather than dying. They know the party is here.
- The six [[Crystal Vials]] are still empty. The waters are still ahead, below the city, and still must never touch Pug's skin.
- The [[Silver Brooch]] remains unused.
- The party now carries the [[Harper Compass]], which points unfailingly at [[Myth Drannor]]. By the escort's reckoning, they'll reach the city's edge by midday tomorrow.
- [[Aurelien Voxs Journal|Aurelien Vox's journal]] warns, in no uncertain terms, that the ruins do not respect maps or "returns", and that a Harper named Aranis was lost beneath the temple of [[Sehanine Moonbow]]. Worth remembering the moment the navigation starts feeling wrong.
- [[Nick Timoney]] still won't fly into a dead-magic zone, and there's a confirmed one at the ravine crossing behind them.
- Herschel now travels with a spore-thrall named Snotty. This will presumably be fine.
