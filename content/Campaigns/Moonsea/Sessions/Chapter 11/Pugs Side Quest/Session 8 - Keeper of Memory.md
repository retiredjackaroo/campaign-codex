_Chapter 11, Side Quest, Session 8_

## A Night at the Edge of the Garden

With the [[Moonstone]] dormant and the [[Moon Deva]] warning them away from the [[Temple of Sehanine Moonbow]], the escort did what they had agreed. One disc came back out of the fountain, the Moonstone woke to guard the temple in their absence, and the companions turned to the rest of [[Myth Drannor]]. The city had to judge them, and it would not do that while they stood in the [[Moon Plaza]].

Their bodies had other ideas first. Nobody had slept properly since the fight for the fountain. They talked briefly about bedding down in the plaza itself, until [[Pug DAstibar|Pug D'Astibar]] pointed out that the deva had told them to hurry, and that unrolling blankets in front of it would be a fairly pointed insult. Instead they walked back to the southern edge of the [[Garden of Long Memory]], which they were almost certain they had cleared, and set a watch.

The elves took most of it. [[Herschel Blackroot]], [[Morwyn Sabbath]], [[Ripper]] and [[Mar]] each kept a turn, while Pug slept as quickly as he could. Nothing came for them, but the night was not quiet.

On every watch, from somewhere in the garden behind them, came the occasional rattle of chains and, after it, a very low laugh.

Mar heard worse to the southeast: the clip-clop of hooves on cobblestones and the crack of whips, but where there should have been the scream of horses there were people, crying out under the lash.

Ripper heard chanting to the south, not the hymns of priests but the oaths warriors swear at their nightly rites. The [[House of Song]] and the Hall of Knights both lay in that direction.

Morwyn, whose watch fell directly south of the camp, heard the most beautiful female voice he had ever heard. It was operatic, not so much words as notes, each one struck perfectly.

Over breakfast, Morwyn told everyone about it at length. Herschel had heard nothing. “You need a musical ear for this stuff first,” said Morwyn. Mar observed that it sounded a great deal better than the whips. Herschel asked whether anyone had heard anything from the north, and when the answer was no, said that was good, because the north was where they were going.

## Past the Noble Quarter

Pug had a preference. The city wanted someone to honour its memory, and Pug's instincts pointed him towards scholarship: the [[Hall of Scrolls]], the Noble Quarter, or above all the High Mage Quarter. Herschel put it simply. They could go anywhere, so they might as well go where Pug was drawn. Pug chose the High Mage Quarter.

They skirted the Moon Plaza, where the Moonstone sat quietly in its mount over a cracked and dry fountain and there was no sign of the deva, then turned north along the [[Grand Avenue]]. Mar scouted ahead. The Hall of Knights stood silent, a crumbling ruin with no hint of the chanting Ripper had heard. The Noble Quarter beyond was terraced and choked with trees and hedges, and a strong smell of rot hung over it. High on its exposed terraces something seemed to move, but it was never more than a shadow shifting, and nothing came down to meet them.

The rest of Myth Drannor showed its damage: scorch marks, rubble, small craters, buildings intact but falling apart. Past the Noble Quarter the damage became something else entirely. Almost nothing stood. Only the skeletal frames of buildings remained, the ground was broken and scorched, and in places red embers still smoked out of it. At its heart rose a great crystalline tree. Ripper suggested they go carefully, so as not to have something else drop on top of them, and the escort crept forward as quietly as a loudmouthed company can.

## The Quarter Remembers

![[Campaigns/Moonsea/Assets/High Mage Quarter - Living Memory.jpg]]

> [!caption] Tel'Mythar in its golden age
> For one breath, Pug saw the [[High Mage Quarter]] whole again.

As Pug crossed into the [[High Mage Quarter]], the ruin vanished. For one breath the world was nothing but silver light, and then the light gathered itself into towers, bridges and soaring halls of white stone under a brilliant blue sky. The Mythal shone at the centre of it all. He felt it before he saw it, an immense presence woven through every stone and every living thing, flowing through the quarter like a second atmosphere. Runes shimmered along the walls. Fountains poured water that hung in the air before falling upwards into floating basins.

Here, magic was not feared but taught. Elves in deep blue robes argued beside humans bent over spell diagrams. A dwarf adjusted a brass instrument ringed by orbiting crystals. Two apprentices bickered over the pronunciation of an incantation, and a senior mage coaxed a nervous student through shaping a flame into a tiny golden bird. On a platform hung between two towers, a circle of archmages wove a working of light that briefly lit the whole quarter, and the Mythal answered with a gentle pulse. Nobody applauded. It was simply another day's work.

In the largest courtyard, beneath a flowering tree, an old mage had set aside her formal robes to show a ring of children a simple cantrip, sparks of coloured light that turned into butterflies. Then she looked up, straight at Pug, and the sounds of the courtyard fell away. She smiled like someone who had been waiting a very long time.

“Remember this,” she said. “Magic was never the glory of Myth Drannor. We were its stewards.”

The children dissolved into silver motes, the towers faded and the ruin returned. For a moment longer Pug could still feel the Mythal, like the warmth of a hand that has only just let go. One last thought settled into his mind.

_What is preserved must be passed on. What is powerful must be made to serve. And what is lost can still be remembered._

## The Bedside of a Dying Guardian

![[Campaigns/Moonsea/Assets/High Mage Quarter - Map.png]]

> [!caption] The High Mage Quarter
> The ruined quarter, with the Heartstone of the Mythal at its centre.

The devastation of the quarter sloped down towards a single point. At the bottom of a vast crater sat the [[Heartstone of the Mythal]]: a pale moonstone at least thirty feet across, half sunk in the earth like the crown of a skull. From it rose a twisting trunk of silver-white crystal, branching into limbs that bore glowing moonflowers. As the escort watched, a flower loosened and drifted to the ground, then another, then another. Thousands lay already on the marble around it, none of them withered.

The Heartstone was wounded. Fractures glowed through its trunk like cracks in frozen moonlight. Whole limbs had sheared away. Its crystal roots spread out beneath the ruined city, some whole, some splintered, some cut cleanly off as though something unimaginably violent had torn them from the earth. Still it lived. Its light moved through the surviving roots slow and steady, like a failing heartbeat.

For the first time since entering Myth Drannor, the strange wrongness that clung to every spell simply stopped. Everything was still. The escort understood that they had not reached the centre of the city's power. They had come to the bedside of a dying guardian.

[[Ocreata]] knew this place, and had spent seven centuries avoiding it. He had been here the day Myth Drannor fell. This was where it started: where the fiends imprisoned above the city came down, and where the first gates opened to let their armies in. He had always known the Mythal was here and glimpsed it in the distance, but to him it had only ever been a smoking crater, the ground zero of the city's destruction. He did not want to go closer, and he could hardly bring himself to look at it.

Herschel studied the tree from a safe distance. The trunk was not wood but limbs of crystal that had grown up out of the stone and twisted together, shaped like one of the forest's great old-growth trees, yet unlike anything he had seen. Pug turned the old mage's words over and wondered aloud whether there was something here they had to preserve, or pass on, or remember. Ocreata noticed a Pool of Far Seeing on the map of the quarter and asked about Pug's [[Crystal Vials]]. The vials, Pug reminded him, were for the pool of radiance below the city, though he did also need a fountain in a Mythal city for a spell of Denlor's. Before doing anything rash, they agreed to learn more.

## The Shattered Observatory

A winding path of broken marble climbed to [[The Shattered Observatory]]. Its dome was gone, leaving great white ribs arching against the sky like the skeleton of some fallen celestial creature. Staircases rose to galleries that no longer existed. Brass rings, shattered lenses and broken instruments lay strewn about, and at the centre stood an enormous circular platform engraved with constellations.

Pug and Mar studied the platform together. It was carved from a single slab of moonstone, and its charts were unlike any astrology Pug knew. There were no predictions and no divinations, only observations: dates, positions, measurements and corrections, recorded by generations of scholars who trusted that the truth, carefully kept, would one day matter to someone they would never meet.

“What is preserved must be passed on,” said Herschel. Ocreata pointed out that writing things down was the entire point of being a wizard. Pug settled in with his calligrapher's tools and his Wizardly Quill and began copying the charts into his books.

Herschel, who preferred to look down rather than up, sifted the debris. The surviving crystal lenses still threw brilliant spectra across the floor, and one band of colour led his eye to a perfectly smooth circle of [[Polished Black Stone]] lying loose near Pug's platform. Unlike the moonstone around it, it reflected nothing. Herschel, drawing on what he called the natural studies of the mycelial network, proposed that it was an optical receiver.

So began the escort's experiment. Ripper held the stone. Ocreata lit a torch. Herschel held a lens over it. The torchlight alone did nothing, but when Ripper looked into the stone it seemed to deepen until it held the sky itself, the stars above Myth Drannor drawn up close, and between them a darkness so deep it made her uneasy. The stone grew warm in her hand. She thought she saw movement.

Herschel did his best to explain. Years ago, a travelling salesman had come to his village selling “moving pictures”: you looked through a lens at engraved plates, and when they shifted, a scene seemed to play out. This, he was sure, was like that. Ripper asked what he was yapping about. Herschel conceded that you had to be there. Ripper shook the stone, waved it about, and promptly made herself very dizzy.

Morwyn took a turn and saw the stars sliding across the stone's surface. He declared it a micro galaxy and handed it to the star druid. Mar found no other stones like it, but at the edge of the platform she identified a meridian pillar, built to catch sunlight and focus it for an instrument that once stood there. Words were carved at its base in ancient Espruar, and Ocreata read them out.

_The heavens need no witness. We watch not for their sake, but for our own._

Mar, steadied by her own Guidance, recognised the constellations in the stone. They were the skies over Myth Drannor and Cormanthor, but not as they stood tonight. The stone showed them at some other time, moving with the real, slow drift of the stars.

When Pug finished his copying and took the stone himself, it flared. The stars that blazed inside it blazed in the engravings on the platform too, then faded one by one in both until only a single constellation remained: the Moonboat. Pug realised the platform was a map of the sky as seen from Myth Drannor. A silver line descended from the Moonboat's brightest star, crossed a map of the city at their feet and ended at the Temple of Sehanine Moonbow. A second point lit up beneath the temple and filled with light until it formed a pool. Script appeared beneath it, and Ocreata translated.

_The heavens mark the hour, the dead bear witness, and the waters receive the promise._

Pug fitted it together with everything the city had shown him. Every memory he had seen came from a single decade, the coronation of the third Coronal, and in the Moon Plaza he had watched the Coronal emerge from the temple newly ascended. A Coronal-to-be, it seemed, had to go beneath the temple and spend time there before taking the throne. Why, and what happened below, he did not know. But the pool was beneath the temple, just as he had suspected.

Ocreata boosted Pug up to the broken gallery and hauled him the rest of the way. There was not much left of it, and Pug was not convinced it would hold them both. Shifting a slab of broken marble, Pug found a weathered but remarkably preserved leather journal. Most of its pages crumbled as he turned them, but it had clearly belonged to an apprentice of the observatory, and one page was still legible.

_Master says every answer creates another question. And tonight, I finally understand why that comforts him._

Pug tucked the [[Observatory Apprentices Journal|apprentice's journal]] into his satchel. Ocreata asked whether he could simply Mending it back together. He could not. Pug was not keen to try Feather Fall with Myth Drannor's magic as it was, so Ocreata climbed down first and caught him.

## Conquerors or Carers

Pug proposed the crater next. Ocreata did not want to go, but said he would anyway. “Courage,” said Pug. “Courage for our friends.”

At the edge of the rift, every small sound of the evening stopped. The Heartstone's branches pulsed in a slow rhythm. Crystal roots rose out of the ground and stopped a few inches from each of their feet, not binding them, simply waiting. Up close, the cracks in the trunk were visibly spreading. Branches were withering and curling back, and moonflowers kept falling.

Then a voice spoke. “Its keepers are gone. Are you here as conquerors, or to assume care?”

“We wish to repair the damage,” said Herschel.

One by one, the escort answered.

Pug asked it directly whether its magic was weak, what it needed to be well again, and whether he could give it magic to heal it. It did not answer, but it pulsed more strongly, and warmth spread out from it.

Herschel stepped close and released his spores, not to harm but to explore, letting them work into the crystal to find what was wrong. The Heartstone pulsed again. Through his spores he understood that this was what had guarded the city and kept it whole, and that the cracks were still growing. Whatever it would take to mend it was beyond him. Ripper leaned over to Pug and said Herschel had done the same thing to her when she had a cough, and that it probably worked better on trees.

Ripper herself tried to read what the tree wanted: whether it wished to go on, or to be put out of its misery. It was asking for neither its end nor its restoration. Perhaps, she sensed, it only wanted to be understood.

Mar spoke of what she had always done, keeping this place safe from those outside, wanting nothing changed or conquered, only kept as it should be. Then she put her hands to the earth. Around the Heartstone, the scar of the Crater of Opening had cut off the little streams that once ran to it from the river. Mar dug and shifted earth, closing the gap, and coaxed a trickle of water back along its old course to the stone.

Ocreata sheathed his glaive and knelt.

“I've sworn an oath before. I've let you and this city down. And I know my words mean little at this point, but know that I will do whatever it takes to keep you safe, keep the city safe, and see that the memories here live on.”

As he spoke of the Mythal's purpose and the obligations of those who lived beneath its protection, he understood what had been lost. That bond had been forgotten. The people of Myth Drannor, the Mage Quarter and the Hall of Knights first among them, had fallen into hubris: the belief that they could not be conquered, that they need not struggle or sacrifice, that they were somehow above it all. The Heartstone pulsed once more, stronger.

Last came Morwyn. He sat down, strung his lute and sang, and as he sang he realised he was not trying to heal it. He was singing to comfort it, to honour the memory of the Mythal and ease its pain for a while.

When the song ended, the Heartstone's pulse grew stronger. The cracks did not close, but their spread slowed. Buds opened on bare branches, one moonflower and then another and then hundreds, and silver light flowed up through the trunk again. They had not restored the Mythal. Perhaps they had reminded it that there were still people worthy of its trust.

The roots around everyone's feet bent towards Pug and curled gently around his. With the next shimmer of the branches he felt, rather than saw, what it had meant to belong to Myth Drannor: children laughing in the plaza, knights swearing oaths, artists performing in the House of Song, librarians in the Hall of Scrolls preserving names, counsellors in the Noble Quarter's forum listening to an orator before speaking themselves, gardeners planting trees throughout the city.

“Stone falls, songs fade, and kingdoms end,” said the voice. Then a long silence. “But memory survives.”

As the roots withdrew into the ground, it added one last thing. “Only if someone chooses to carry it.”

The instability that had dogged them since they entered the city eased. A calm settled over the crater, and the fallen moonflowers opened a little wider and shone a little brighter. The bleeding had been staunched. In his mind, Pug heard a title.

_Keeper of Memory._

The escort debated what it meant. Herschel thought it called for acceptance, a promise to do something with what Pug had been shown. Pug wondered how a promise could be made real, and to whom. Ocreata warned that it would be worth finding out what the title entailed before he ended up trapped here forever, remembering things. Morwyn suggested a song. Pug said he was more of a prose man. Ripper suggested Pug write it all down and let Morwyn turn it into a song or a play.

In the end Pug said it plainly. He accepted the title and the responsibility of being the Keeper of Memory, and would try to keep the memory of Myth Drannor. Nothing further answered him.

## The Great Way Circle

At [[The Great Way Circle]], pale stone rings radiated from a flawless centre like ripples on still water, each carved with thousands of spiralling elven runes. Infernal fire had split whole sections into fissures, and broken arches and pillars of a graceful colonnade leaned over the platform. Beneath the surviving glyphs a blue-white glow lingered. Every so often a rune flickered to life, then another, before the light died. The place felt as though it remembered countless arrivals and farewells, as though someone might still step through if you waited long enough.

Pug recognised a teleportation circle whose light could no longer run its full course: wherever runes were damaged or missing, the flow simply stopped. Herschel read the runes. There were seven concentric rings, each standing for a principle the High Mages held necessary before anyone journeyed beyond their home: Curiosity, Humility, Wisdom, Fellowship, Responsibility, Trust and Return. The outer rings were badly damaged; the innermost were almost perfect. The circle kept trying to complete its activation and failing, never in the same sequence twice. Most of its runes did not say where it led, but how one ought to travel.

Herschel and Mar spotted two discs set into the circle, engraved with the sigils of Semberholme and Tangled Trees, the other cities of the elven kingdom of Cormanthyr, both believed reduced to ruins. Repairing the circle would take more than one mage, a great deal of time, or both, and even then they could not be sure it would be stable. The escort left it as they found it, apart from one stone, which Pug pocketed. He was collecting components for a set of Denlor's half-completed spells, and one of them called for a stone from three teleportation circles.

Ocreata volunteered to embody Return, on the grounds that he was stuck here.

## The Collapsed Crystal Conservatory

Next door, [[Collapsed Crystal Conservatory|the Collapsed Crystal Conservatory]] rose from the ruins like the delicate skeleton of a fallen giant. Its arches of white stone still traced a vast vaulted hall. Crystal panes larger than a ship's sail lay broken across the floor, while others hung cracked in their frames, throwing rainbows everywhere. Beneath them, nature had quietly taken over: wild gardens, moss over the paths, vines climbing the broken ribs as though trying to mend them, petrified trees gleaming like ivory. The place was not restored, only patient.

Ocreata remembered it. It had been one of Tel'Mythar's most beloved institutions: not a greenhouse but a living archive of magical plants gathered from every corner of Toril and beyond, every species documented and preserved for those who would come after. Every apprentice tended these gardens, whatever their magical ability, because the High Mages believed magic should never be divorced from the natural world. Scholars came here to think rather than to experiment. Even now the Heartstone's influence nourished the soil, and a few extraordinary plants still survived among the ordinary ones.

The escort spread out. In the evening light, the surviving canopy turned silver. Channels in the marble floor still carried trickles of impossibly clear water diverted from the river. At the centre stood a ring of ancient trees, one with silver leaves that chimed in the breeze, another beginning to bloom as moonlight touched it, and before them a stone meant for kneeling.

_To tend one life well is to honour all life._

Beneath a sheltered section of roof, a climbing vine bloomed with silver moonflowers, living ones, impossibly healthy.

Herschel began taking cuttings, very carefully. Ripper spotted a carved stone cabinet half buried under fallen crystal. Its drawers held dozens of tiny crystal containers, most empty, a handful holding [[Ancient Seeds]]. Ripper announced that they seemed useless, then handed them to Herschel and told him to preserve them. Herschel, who considered himself something of a horticulturist, could not tell whether they were still viable and could not place a single species. He had never seen seeds like them. Nothing growing in the conservatory now matched them either; the living plants were the forest's ordinary species, which had moved in over the centuries. He tucked the seeds away to plant somewhere, someday.

Pug followed one of the water channels to where it entered the conservatory and lifted a crystal pane. Beneath it sat a [[Bronze Purifying Vessel|bronze vessel with a spout]]. River water flowed in, not especially clean, and came out pristine. Pug picked it up. The garden did not wilt, but the water in the channels turned back into plain river water.

The escort argued over whether to take it. Ocreata did not like the idea of ruining the conservatory, even to help the Heartstone. Herschel thought the plants here were common enough to survive anywhere, merely thriving on the purified water. In the end Pug kept the vessel, on the understanding that he would bring it back if they found no better use for it, perhaps at the Heartstone or at the Pool of Far Seeing, which they had not yet visited.

## What the City Is Asking

Herschel noticed one last thing. None of the plants competed. Vines did not choke the trees they climbed. Flowers bloomed without crowding their neighbours. Saplings grew in the shelter of older trees. In nature, that simply did not happen.

“Humility, wisdom, fellowship, responsibility, trust and return,” said Ripper.

Herschel voiced his frustration: what did any of it mean? As he asked, the pieces began to fall into place for several of them. Everything they had encountered in Myth Drannor was a vestige of what the city had once been, and all of it pointed the same way: stewardship, a shared existence, the humility to believe no one part mattered more than the whole. That had been the city's real strength, and perhaps that was what it was asking them to remember.

Ocreata saw why it mattered so much. It was exactly what Myth Drannor had lost before it fell. Nobles who did not share the city's philosophy had gained influence. Elves had come to see themselves as first among those taken into the knightly orders and the academy. Archmages had begun to pursue power for its own sake rather than for what it gave the city. The Knights had been so certain they would defeat the enemy that little was done to protect the people, and when they lost, far more died than had to.

Pug said it had been hubris that brought Myth Drannor down, and wondered aloud whether the third Coronal, the one who went below the temple, had been part of it. Nobody yet knew. They still had to work out how to act on any of it.
