# Escort to Myth Drannor

_Side Quest, Session 1_

## **Part One: The Tower Door**

Pugnacious D'Astibar had told the party he had personal business to attend to near [[Denlors Tower|Denlor's Tower]]. That much was even true, as far as it went.

The rest of it had taken him well out of Phlan: secreted away from his own clan, along the Iron Road, through two ferry crossings, and finally into [[Shadowdale]], travel-worn and quietly hoping nobody important would ask too many questions about why he looked like he'd slept in a hedge.

![[Campaigns/Moonsea/Assets/Shadowdale.webp]]

He adjusted his robes as best he could, knocked the worst of the dirt from his hair, and raised a hand to knock on the tower door.

The door swung open before his knuckles landed.

Inside, an old wizard waited by the hearth, a beard magnificent enough to deserve its own title, a weathered blue hat hanging from a peg nearby. He studied Pug over the rim of a chipped teacup for a long moment.

"You're Pugnacious," he said, and let the name hang there. "Did your parents lose a wager?"

Pug allowed that it was a family name.

A woman's voice cut in from empty air before he could say much else. "Oh, hush, El. At least his name warns people what they're getting." And then she was simply there, a translucent figure in flowing silver robes, moonlight gathering around her though it was only afternoon outside. "Greetings, young Pug. I am Syl, and despite my appearance, not that Syl."

Elminster, for it was Elminster, huffed that he'd been called worse that morning already.

![[Campaigns/Moonsea/Assets/Elminster.png]]

Pug asked, with the particular earnestness of someone trying very hard to be polite in a room that clearly didn't require it, whether the tower belonged to Syl as well, or just to Elminster.

That got him a swat and a blush. "You certainly had your chance to make it yours, didn't you?" Elminster said, entirely too pleased with himself. "You're never going to grow up, are you?" was as much answer as he got, before Elminster grabbed a plate stacked absurdly high with cake, three cakes' worth by the look of it all piled onto one plate, and shoved it towards their guest.

"I'm an optimist," he said, when Syl arched an eyebrow at him.

Pug ate. Between mouthfuls he was interrogated on nothing in particular: how long he'd studied alchemy, whether he'd ever been chased by an angry goose (vampires, recently, but no geese, though he'd keep an eye out), whether he'd ever set his own eyebrows on fire (often, since taking up alchemy, which Elminster took as evidence of good character), what had frightened him most (a demon leaping out of a chest he really shouldn't have touched). Syl asked what book had first made him believe magic was beautiful. Elminster called it a boring question before Pug could even answer it.

Then, more quietly, Syl asked what gave him hope. Pug talked about Phlan, about ordinary people banding together against the Maimed Virulence when the monsters came. Neither of them commented. Neither of them needed to.

Elminster went rummaging through the bookshelves for something that, by the sound of the crash that followed, shouldn't have been in the tower at all. He returned with a polished walnut case and set it on the table.

![[Campaigns/Moonsea/Assets/Crystal Vials.png]]

Inside, six crystal vials nestled in blue velvet, each marked with faint silver runes. "These remain for one purpose," he said. "If the waters you seek truly remain what they are, they'll not stay that way for long. These will persuade them otherwise." He held one out, then pulled it straight back and snapped the case shut the instant Pug reached for it.

Syl's hand, when she offered it, was solid; the moment she took the brooch from around her own neck and passed it to him, it stopped being translucent entirely. Warm. Real. "The creatures of Cormanthor remember more than most people believe," she told him, fastening it to his cloak herself. "When they see this, they will know you arrive as a friend before a stranger. And should you ever truly require aid, place a hand on it and ask politely."

"Politer manners than most adventurers," Elminster muttered, filling his pipe, before adding, almost as an afterthought, that Pug should probably get walking. Myth Drannor lay due east of the village. There were old game trails through the forest, if he looked for them.

"If the Lord Sage's assistants are anything like the assignments he sends them on," Elminster said, stroking his beard, "they're either remarkably capable, or he's simply run out of people he's fond of." Asked whether that meant the journey was dangerous, he considered it. "No, I'd never call it dangerous. I'd call it a pleasant stroll that usually ends with someone writing songs, because there was no one left to write complaints."

He raised his teacup. "Now, finish your cake. The forest's waiting."

## **Part Two: Alone in the Rimwood**

Shadowdale's last farms gave way to two worn ruts through scattered pines, and then to a weathered stone marking the edge of Cormanthor. Pug summoned Nick Timoney to ride his shoulder as he went, and struck out along the old road.

![[Campaigns/Moonsea/Assets/Cormanthor.webp]]

For the first while it was pleasant enough: wide-spaced pines, needle-carpeted ground, sunlight in broad shafts, the kind of silence that felt expectant rather than empty. Then, without any obvious cause, the forest simply stopped. The birdsong cut off. Something in the undergrowth clicked, like pine needles snapping. Pug sent Nick Timoney up into the high branches and turned to look.

The path behind him was gone. Not blocked. Gone, roots and saplings and bushes knitted across it as though it had never existed. A Detect Magic turned up a faint aura of transmutation clinging to the tangle. Pug called out to whatever was watching him, invited it to stop hiding, walked right up to the bushes to prove he wasn't afraid. Nothing answered. Nick Timoney, sent to scout from above, reported only that the woods felt watched, though she couldn't say by what.

There was nothing for it but to keep walking, so he did, glancing back every so often and trying to look like a man who had simply decided to carry on.

Then the pine needles started falling. One landed on his shoulder and stuck. Another on his chest. One became ten, ten became dozens, all of them clinging to his robes and pricking through the fabric until moving got genuinely difficult. He tried to hurry. The needles piled faster than he could shed them. He drew a dagger and scraped a clump free of his shoulder, only for the needles to transfer their allegiance to the blade instead, which promptly became too heavy to be worth carrying. He dropped it, tried to break into a run, and went sprawling flat on his face for his trouble, needles now settling across his back as well.

Levitate seemed like the answer. Twenty feet up, pushing off tree trunks to move himself along, well clear of whatever was falling from above. It worked, more or less, all the way to the edge of a ravine, where a brook fell fifty feet down mossy stone steps and a rope bridge, elegant, elven, clearly the only way across, waited on the other side of the gap. The moment he reached the treeline the needles fell away entirely, and the Levitate spell, entirely without his permission, began lowering him gently back to earth as though it too had decided it was done with him.

He crossed the bridge on foot. Halfway over, the ropes didn't break so much as simply let go, untying themselves as though they'd never been ropes at all, and the whole structure came apart into a hanging curtain of vines. Pug's instinct was to cast Feather Fall. His better instinct, arriving a half-second later, was to just grab on, and an excellent bit of acrobatics saw him swing the rest of the way across, wrapped in vines like some particularly bookish version of Tarzan.

Nick Timoney did not follow him across the ravine. Whatever had swallowed the bridge, the owl wanted no part of it, and Pug, dusting himself off on the far side, filed that away for later: somewhere back there was a patch of Cormanthor that even his own familiar wouldn't fly into.

## **Part Three: The Guardians of Mithranor**

He didn't get long to compose himself. A woman in plate armour stepped out of the bushes to meet him, a shield and a whip at her belt, and told him without an ounce of apology that whatever some of the others had thought, she'd found the whole performance funny as hell.

It was only then it became clear: the vanishing path, the pine needles, the bridge that dissolved into vines. All of it had been a test, watched the entire time by the very people who were meant to be escorting him, none of whom had lifted a finger to help.

An elf paladin trailing behind her looked faintly ill about the whole business. "Was the prank meant to kill this guy?" he asked nobody in particular. "He falls to his death, what a jolly time." A centaur emerged next, deadpan, wanting to know how they were all meant to fetch Pug's dagger back now that he'd flung it into the pine trees. Then a voice from what looked like an unremarkable patch of undergrowth, until the undergrowth turned out to be a chubby, moss-covered elf who'd been standing there in full view the entire time: "Son, what did you think those pine needles were trying to tell you?"

Last of all, a high elf bard in black, lute slung with a skull at its head, arrived with the air of a man arriving fashionably late to his own review. "Come on," he said, "that's one of the lightest exercises I've ever seen. If I were writing this up, I'd call it _the comedy of errors._"

Introductions did not go smoothly. Names were traded, half-heard, and immediately mangled: Ripper, Mar, a green-skinned figure Pug settled on calling Herschel after a few tries, and an elf paladin who insisted on spelling his own name out loud rather than trust anyone to get it right, to no obvious benefit, since within the hour Pug had already tried Kion and Bjorn before something like Kjeorn stuck. The bard introduced himself as Morwen and announced he'd be taking notes, since evidently nobody else had thought to.

They were, Ripper explained, the guardians of Mithranor: several different groups, Knights among them, though as one of them pointed out, they preferred the daytime to living up to the name. The centaur's people were Sylvan warriors, not, Morwen clarified for Pug's benefit, a taxi service, though you were welcome to think of it that way provided you tipped well and didn't upset her.

Pug, relieved beyond measure to have actual company for once, launched into an unprompted comparison with his old adventuring party back in Phlan, warming to the subject in a way that made the escort visibly uncomfortable. He was fairly sure this lot would pull their weight far better than that old crowd had, he said, several times, with the particular emphasis of a man trying to convince himself as much as anyone else. Nobody quite dared ask him outright whether the old party had survived. Ripper's read on it, delivered quietly to the others, was that they sounded like a bunch of useless pricks, which was as tactful as anyone managed to be about it.

Someone asked, with real suspicion, whether Pug had anything overtly red about him, on the theory that a wizard walking into Myth Drannor unannounced might be Red Wizard trouble. Pug, entirely in brown from boots to backpack, was cleared of the charge and cheerfully likened to Radagast, a comparison he seemed to take as a compliment.

Morwen, scenting an opportunity, offered to immortalise the whole misadventure in song, for a fee, on the grounds that at this point Pug badly needed the publicity. Pug allowed that he'd love to hear it, provided the fee stayed reasonable, which Morwen did not promise.

## **Part Four: Into Cormanthor**

![[Campaigns/Moonsea/Assets/Your New Friends.webp]]

They set out together, the escort falling into a practised formation around him without needing to discuss it. Nick Timoney, once safely back over the ravine, resumed her scouting overhead, and the party pressed on into the forest proper.

![[Campaigns/Moonsea/Assets/The Journey Begins.png]]

Hours in, without any obvious boundary, the forest changed again. The pines gave way to broad ash and beech, the light softened from harsh gold to filtered emerald, and the ground beneath grew rich with moss and moon fern and wild honeysuckle. Deer watched from between the trunks and did not run.

![[Campaigns/Moonsea/Assets/Cormanthor - The Midwood.png]]

Herschel ranged off the path in his boots of elvenkind, utterly silent except for one badly timed lapse that nobody mentioned twice. The rest kept loosely to pairs, ten feet apart, chatting when the mood allowed it, until Nick Timoney, sent ahead to scout, reported back: a small glade up ahead, choked with fallen, moss-covered trees, and a group of upright, boar-faced creatures already at work butchering a stag.

## **Part Five: The Glade**

They found the glade before they meant to.

![[Campaigns/Moonsea/Assets/Cormanthor - The Stalkers.png]]

Six upright, boar-faced shapes stood over the carcass of a stag, already well into the business of butchering it, with two much larger boars standing guard nearby. A pause, a handful of Nature and History checks, and the party had their answer: were-boars, lycanthropes bound to Malar, and not the kind of thing you wanted to stumble into by accident.

The debate that followed was short and half-hearted. Some wanted to go around. Pug wanted to keep moving towards the shrine and not spend a fight he didn't need to have. The consensus, such as it was, leaned towards slipping past.

The boars had other ideas.

Two of the giant boars broke from the treeline first, and the armed were-boars came in behind them a heartbeat later. The DM called for initiative with advantage across the board, which was at least some small mercy. For a moment or two, half the party lost their sight entirely: a technical glitch that blacked Pug's vision and another player's along with it, resolved after an uncomfortable pause in which nobody was quite sure whether it was in-fiction or out.

Then the fight was very much real.

Herschel planted Spike Growth across a twenty-foot stretch of ground, turning it into a field of thorns that would punish anything careless enough to cross it. Pug caught a shaman mid-incantation and shut the lightning spell down cold with Counterspell, then answered with a Chromatic Orb of his own, lightning arcing from a green were-boar into a purple one behind it for sixty-eight points and then nineteen more. Mar lit up the shaman with Starry Wisp, ten points of radiant damage and a dim, unwelcome glow that wouldn't let him hide, then reached for Shillelagh to turn an ordinary quarterstaff into something considerably less ordinary.

Morwen handed a d8 of Bardic Inspiration to a teammate who badly needed it, tried Cutting Words on an incoming attack and watched it land anyway, and then threw Faerie Fire across the glade, outlining several of the were-boars in blue, green and violet light that made them very easy to hit. Kjeorn drove a Thunderous Smite home for good measure, thunder rolling off the blow, then followed it with a Shield Bash that put a boar flat on its back.

Ripper found the red were-boar with her whip and cracked it across the joint with enough force to slow it down and leave it fighting at a disadvantage from then on, muttering something about old habits. The boars didn't take that lying down. One of them charged her outright and hit hard, forty-seven points of damage in a single blow, and she went down prone, badly hurt, the worst any of them had taken all session.

Spike Growth kept doing its work in the background, chewing through anything that tried to cross it, damage rolling in twos and fours of d4s as boars and party alike misjudged where the thorns actually were.

The fight was still going when the session ended: were-boars down but not finished, Ripper down and hurt, Pug's crystal vials still empty in their case, the silver brooch still unused on his cloak, and the shrine, and whatever waited in its pool, still some distance further into Cormanthor.

## **Loose Ends**

The six crystal vials haven't been filled yet. The water still hasn't touched Pug's skin, and by all accounts it needs to stay that way.

The silver brooch has been received, not used. Whoever or whatever it's meant to be shown to hasn't turned up yet.

Nick Timoney won't follow into a dead-magic zone. There's at least one such patch back along the route, right at the ravine crossing, worth remembering if the shrine turns out to be another.

Ripper is down, hurt badly, and the were-boars are still standing. However this ends, it isn't over yet.

And somewhere ahead, past the Rimwood and the mid-wood and whatever's left of the glade once the fighting stops, an old elven shrine is waiting on a pool of water that a very old wizard was extremely specific about nobody touching.
