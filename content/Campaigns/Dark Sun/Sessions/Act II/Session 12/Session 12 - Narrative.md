## The Road Beneath Raam

[[Leviath]] led the party out of [[The Hidden Spring]] in the opposite direction from the old Neogi lair, deeper beneath [[Raam]] itself. The path through the dark was slick with condensation, the porous stone walls beaded with moisture that vanished into the sand underfoot. For most of a full day they followed her, until the tunnel opened into a broad cavern lit not by flame but by luminous mats of pale green growth clinging to the walls. At its centre lay a pool, rippling faintly and throwing back the fungal glow, so that the whole chamber seemed to breathe.

"Always a pool," muttered [[Anselem]].

![[Session 12 - The Masters Pool.webp]]
_The cavern of the Master's pool, deep beneath Raam._

Waiting there, as though she had always been part of the place, stood [[Master Avariah]]. She was tall, cloaked in flowing desert silks that shimmered like water in moonlight, with the smooth skin and timeless features of someone who had preserved herself against the decay of Athas. Her eyes shone with an almost celestial light, and beneath it lay something sharper. She carried a sceptre of bone wound in a spiral of obsidian, its head cradling a dark crystal in which a faint flicker of firelight pulsed like a heartbeat.

"Children of Raam," she said, spreading her arms, her voice soft and melodic and carrying an authority that never needed to rise. "I have heard whispers of your deeds. You stood within the shadow of the Padshah's fall and walked away, tempered but unbroken. You are rare gifts in this age."

She bade them sit on woven reed mats set in a semicircle around the glowing pool. A clay brazier burned beside it, filling the air with the scent of spice and smoke. Anselem asked what she was burning; it was something floral, with notes of rosewood and lavender. [[Tchk Tchk Gulbadani|Tchk'Tchk]] settled on his mat. [[Salt]] went politely to sit down. [[Derfel]] stayed standing at the back, leaning against the wall, with no intention of getting comfortable.

## Salt

As Salt passed her, Avariah touched him very gently on the shoulder. "Forgive me. I would like to be properly introduced to you."

"My name is Salt."

"Salt," she repeated, looking at him with something close to reverence. "Quite ironic, isn't it? Named after the most plentiful resource in this world, yet you are one of its rarest beings, aren't you?"

Salt looked like a rabbit caught in the light. He managed an awkward nod.

"There is something about you," she went on. "Something of the living world that this desert has tried so very hard to extinguish. I have spent much of my life studying old accounts of Athas, the world before, the powers that shaped it, and the mysteries they failed to master. Most of the records are fragments, hints and possibilities that could be argued by a scholar or a madman. Occasionally, however, one encounters something that makes the old questions seem suddenly very urgent." She caught herself. "Peace. Forgive me, I am getting caught up. Please, join your friends."

Salt scurried to sit beside [[Pterrence]], who patted him on the shoulder. Avariah stepped into the middle of the semicircle. She was most pleased the party had found the Alliance, she said, and her gaze settled once more on Salt: she did not believe that to be an accident. Then she asked them to explain the matter that pressed on them above.

## The Water Beneath Raam

Pterrence told her plainly that they wanted to rescue their friend [[Korno]], and ideally stop [[The Templars]] taking over Raam through the cult of Dregoth. He added that the Templars might have secret weapons buried in the vaults beneath their stronghold. Avariah's eyes rose at that: she knew of the vaults beneath the Ivory Palace. What lay there, she said, might be construed as weapons, in that the old powers had judged it too dangerous to be left in the hands of the people.

Of Korno she spoke with kindness and restraint. Leviath had long been the Alliance's friend, and she knew what Korno meant to the people of Raam. His fate pained her. But he was not an ally of theirs, and his path was not the path of the five aims. "Our strength is not for scattering on every rebel voice crying out in the waste. We must choose carefully where we risk our lives and our cause."

From a satchel at her side she drew a stone tablet etched with flowing ancient script and laid it reverently on the mat. It was one of many, she said, salvaged from beneath the Ivory Palace. It told of a great mortal, the first master of magic as it was meant to be, a man she named Rajaat: a creator of the Athas they knew, who had mastered pure, preserving, limitless magic in the age when Athas was a paradise. There were clues in such tablets that the true key to power lay not in destruction but in sustaining, which ought to be impossible on a dying world.

Her voice grew more intent. The water beneath Raam, she said, was not what House Gulbadan had told the people. It did not dwindle. It did not fail as other wells did. She had come to believe it was fed by a source that must be tremendous. "You were all once vassals of House Gulbadan. Is that correct?"

They had been. Babs had been taken into the house right before the fall. Derfel observed that some of them had been more Gulbadan than others and looked pointedly at Tchk'Tchk, who had taken the name as his own and been all in on it "for the week that it lasted". Derfel added that it had been a fantastic way of identifying the flag-wavers in the party. Avariah told them what they already knew: the estate lay in ruin. But she believed secrets remained there.

That brought back the tapestry. [[Babs]] reminded the party of it: the great hanging in the Gulbadan house that mapped the aqueducts beneath the whole city. Every line on it ran to a water source, except the lines feeding the [[Gulbadan Estate]] itself, which ran off the edge of the map. [[The Padshah]] had shown it to them with pride, as the family's gift to Raam, and told them not to read too much into what she called artistic licence.

"She may very well have been lying," Avariah said. "Perhaps we can only hope that there is a record, a design, some truth that they hid from all of us. If you truly wish to aid not just Raam but all of Athas, if you wish to bring hope to this land, then I ask you: go to the Gulbadan estate and find this secret. That is where your true path lies. Not throwing yourselves upon Templar blades for Korno, whose loyalty to the aims is unproven. If you truly want to subvert the Templars, take away the one control that keeps everyone supplicant. Help us find this everlasting source, and we will unleash it and free all of Raam. Not just one person."

Then her voice rang out as if she were no longer speaking to them at all: "Remember the five aims. To preserve life. To oppose defilers. To protect knowledge. To safeguard the hidden. And to train the next generation." The Gulbadan estate, she said, touched upon preservation and the safeguarding of secrets. Korno did not. "Do not be distracted by sentiment. Do not squander what our alliance has built."

## The Argument

"We're being given a binary choice, gang," said Tchk'Tchk.

Pterrence argued that if they helped Korno, Korno could help them. Derfel, hating to agree with Pterrence, said they owed Korno. Avariah allowed each of them to make their case.

Pterrence asked whether, if they learned more, they might find a way to restore life to Athas. Of course that was possible, Avariah said; was it something they were willing to risk their lives for? Pterrence said yes, they would sacrifice themselves for it. Salt nodded along. When Pterrence suggested they could search the vaults while they were in the Templar district freeing Korno, Avariah corrected him: what she was asking was that they go to the Gulbadan estate.

Salt pressed her. Letting the Templars sacrifice a crowd of people in a grand show would be good for nobody's aims, he said, and what was her plan for saving anyone beyond a water source that might or might not exist? It did not matter whether Korno himself achieved anything: the people saw him as important, and so did the Templars. Avariah answered that everyone on the surface lived under someone's control because someone else controlled the water. If an undying source were unearthed and unleashed, what need would the people have to live as slaves to the Templars? Or the party could free Korno, and he could go on with his little resistance in the slums. "It was going so well before the storm. He almost took the city. He almost defeated the Templars. No." Salt's plea did not move her. She put a hand on his shoulder: she understood the goodness of his heart, but if they were to lead, they would have to absorb such hurts.

Derfel asked her straight out whether the Templars were her allies. "Of course not. The Templars are defilers." Then did she want to face them at full strength, he asked, or weakened? Korno was a thorn in their side as long as he lived. If what she believed lay beneath Raam was real, Avariah replied, no power the Templars could achieve would be able to oppose it. Derfel made her say it: the endless spring of water. Even then, Derfel argued, she would have to defend it, forever if the Templars stayed strong. Babs stood beside him, nodding at every point. Avariah said they misunderstood her. She did not seek to control the water. If it existed, she meant to unleash it as a truth the Templars could not deny, so that the people would know they need not be tethered. Leviath had always preached self-determination. Had Korno given that to his people? Or had he ruled a scrap of land he could not even protect? Derfel shot back that Korno had been about to strike the Templars very effectively when the Inundation came. "And you accused me of speaking in maybes," she said to Salt.

[[Kit-Chac]] spoke last of those who chose to: "Water for all is a dream for the soft-shelled. Korno is pack. Pack is now. First we take him back. Then we hunt your dream, if it's real." Avariah said she understood: he spoke of Korno as blood of his blood. But if they were to liberate the many, there were sacrifices that would weigh on them forever, and who was to say Korno was not one of them?

Tchk'Tchk wondered whether he could simply teleport Korno out of there himself, and that it might be possible to split up and do both. He hoped his magic was strong enough, Avariah said, to pass whatever wards and watchers the Templars had set on a prisoner like Korno; Tchk'Tchk's own instincts told him there were surely powerful psionicists in Templar service who had thought of that. Anselem, asked whether he wished to add anything, declined; he would take whatever mission was put in front of him.

Avariah saw that it still weighed on them. That was admirable, she said, and rather inconvenient. "We need you to be thinking with your minds."

Babs studied her closely while the others talked. Every faction leader she had met had wanted power, and this one was asking them to hand her the greatest power source on Athas. To all appearances Avariah was human, tall and older than she looked, perhaps with a trace of elven blood in ears that were large but not quite elven. But everything she had said had been about finding the water and releasing it, never about holding it. At the same time Derfel snapped his fingers, calling his companion [[Grave]] to his side.

## Terms

Derfel had had enough of city politics. He told her bluntly that the party was independent and would do as it pleased, and asked what she would do if they went to save Korno. Avariah answered that she had no intention of being an authority over them. But if they left to free their friend, she would have to move her people out of the Hidden Spring, because the party would be endangering them with what they knew. She would carry on her work, perhaps without their help.

Derfel let her have it plainly. They had friends, and those friends had helped them in the Hidden Spring. They did not trust institutions; they trusted people. Korno had helped them. They were not against her, or against anyone except the Templars, a couple of Nightrunners and a few others, and the rest of the party cheerfully chimed in with everyone else they had made enemies of. "As you can see," Derfel finished, "we're not unused to enemies, and we're still here. We'd prefer to be friends, and we'd like to keep our friends alive. I don't see these as mutually exclusive. We should save Korno, and we should help you at the same time."

Avariah raised her hand in a calming gesture. "Friends, friends." She agreed with almost all of it and apologised if she had seemed to command them. Then she set out what would happen. She would relocate the Hidden Spring cell; they had done it many times. But a rescue did not mean the party could not still help her. Leviath had ways to reach her that would not betray her location. As a favour in friendship, she asked one thing: go to the Gulbadan estate first. An attempt on Korno, successful or not, would raise an alarm and make the Templars guard every important site more closely, so the estate, and the vaults if they meant to try them, should come before any rescue. Nor was there any need to snatch Korno in broad daylight before a crowd of guards; he was taken back inside the Templar district each night. If fortune favoured them, they might find the Gulbadan secret, take any remaining tablets from the vault and free Korno before the alarm was raised. Afterwards they should go underground to the pool they had passed on their way to the Hidden Spring and wait, and she would find a way to bring them to a sanctuary beyond the Templars' reach.

The party agreed.

Pterrence fished out the forged work papers [[Chazhal|the Pitch]] had given them and asked whether they would be enough to get into the Templar district. Avariah was no expert, she said; if they had doubts, they should have fresh ones forged by the same Pitch. Asked what else she could offer, she said the Hidden Spring would supply healing potions and whatever weapons it had in its stores. And Leviath had told her that the Padshah's handmaiden still lived: [[Neith]], whom the party had freed. Neith might know more about the estate. She had returned to the slums, and Leviath knew where to find her.

As for the vault, Avariah said she could tell them how her people had got in, but would not try that way again: members of another cell had reached it from underground, by a route that had taken a very long time to find, and the Templars would surely know their vault had been breached. She feared anyone going near it might walk into a trap. That, Derfel and Pterrence agreed, was rather their speciality.

## Salt Stays Behind

"May I have a little time with Salt, alone?"

Salt shot Pterrence a panicked look. At that moment Grave padded into the chamber and sat down between Derfel and Babs. Derfel told Avariah that Grave would stay with Salt, and asked whether she meant to pat the dog or eat him. She got by without eating meat, she said. Salt hugged the dog. "If you hear high-pitched squealing," he told the others, "you'll know there's been an issue."

While they waited outside, the rest of the party worked out its order of business: find Neith first, then the Gulbadan estate, new papers from the Pitch, the vault, and Korno. Pterrence quietly cast Detect Magic as a ritual so that he could check Salt when he came back. When Salt emerged, Pterrence found nothing on him but the magic of the items he already carried.

Salt told them what Avariah had asked. She wanted him to stay behind with the Alliance and help with work involving the springs and the water. She was also worried that he would be swept up and enslaved the moment he set foot above ground, a fear Pterrence shared. There was no pay, unless one counted reputation, but it would go a long way to putting the Alliance firmly on their side. Salt believed she was genuine in her conviction about the water, though he warned that his read on her had not been a good one. She was, he added, much older than any of them had guessed. Salt loved his friends, owed Pterrence, and did not want them walking into danger without him, but by the time the party prepared to leave he had made his choice: he would stay with Avariah. A companion from the Hidden Spring would go up with the party in his place, a quiet elf who served as a cleric of the air.

## The Surface

The Hidden Spring kept its word. Each of them took healing potions brewed from the extracts of local fungus and lichen; Pterrence and Derfel chose two regular draughts over a single greater one. Babs took a simple glaive from the stores, prompting Anselem to admit he had been dragging a spare glaive around ever since he took up his magic sword. Pterrence traded his scale mail for an equivalent breastplate that would not hamper his stealth, made, he decided, of chitin. Before they parted, Salt pressed most of his ceramic on Pterrence, who needed a costly focus for the Clairvoyance he meant to learn and promised to pay him back.

Leviath advised waiting for nightfall, and the party agreed. Under cover of darkness they climbed out of a long-dead well in the arena markets near [[The Furnace]]. It had been many days since they had felt open air on their skin and in their lungs, and there was a lightness to it.

![[Session 12 - A Breath of Fresh Air.png]]
_Raam by night, rebuilt under the Templar yoke._

The arena markets had been cleaned up and repaired since they had last been above ground, but for those who had been at the Furnace when the storm struck, every corner brought back a memory: carts and stalls torn up into the cyclone, walls crumbling, a whole crowd vanishing beneath falling stone. To the north lay [[The Slums]]; to the west the walls of the Templar district, with Korno somewhere beyond; to the south the trade emporium and the quickest route to the noble quarter and the Gulbadan estate.

The streets were quieter than they remembered, and not from peace. The hush was exhaustion and fear. By day much of the populace laboured in the blistering heat, bent over rubble or lashed to work gangs; by evening they trudged in silence, scavenging or searching for water with the dull look of the resigned. Crude shrines of stone and clay had risen in the open spaces, idols warped into dragon-like shapes, the mark of the cult of Dregoth, displayed openly. Acolytes in dark robes called out along the avenues, promising salvation to the faithful and retribution to the disobedient, and many knelt, from belief or to avoid notice. Templar patrols in gleaming bronze armour marched with iron discipline, their ranks swollen by eager converts of [[Dregoth Cultists|Dregoth]], and the crowds parted before them like dust before the wind.

Leviath could go no further with them; with the party she would surely be recognised. Tchk'Tchk briefly wondered whether he could alter her appearance, but Alter Self, as the others pointed out, is in the name. She gave them directions and left them.

The party did their best to look unremarkable. Tchk'Tchk used Alter Self on himself to become the most average person he could imagine. Pterrence put on a fake limp, and Anselem, impressed, started limping too. Derfel simply strode ahead with Grave at his heel and asked whether anyone expected him to disguise his dog as a velociraptor. Kit-Chac blended into his surroundings so completely that he became almost invisible. As the most inconspicuous of them, Kit-Chac listened in as they passed the ruined arena into the slums. Two labourers were grumbling about why the Templars kept such a heavy presence in the slums when the rebels were already beaten and their leader hung from the gates every morning. Because, the other said, there was still food hidden in the slums, guarded by Korno's loyalists.

## Neith

The slums were the part of the city that remained most in ruin. Following Leviath's directions, the party tried asking for the way. People treated them like narcs and fled, but before one group scattered the party overheard them talking about Korno: he had been hung on that wall all day, several days running, and yet each morning he seemed healthy and restored. "I hear they're feeding Korno like a king before he's lashed to that wall. They want him strong for the sacrifice."

Deep in the heart of the slums they found a tent city of scrap wood and hovels lashed together amid the ruins, where most of the surviving population had gathered. In one hovel, shared with several other forced labourers, sat Neith: legs out on the dirt floor, back against the wall, a ceramic cup in her hands and her head bowed. When the party had freed her at the Gulbadan estate she had still been strong and defiant. Now her cheeks were emaciated, her eyes sunken and her hair thinning. But when she raised her head and saw them, her eyes went wide and she smiled.

Pterrence told her, at his ordinary speaking volume, that they had come for her, that they were going to fight the Templars and restore the city, and that they were going back to the Gulbadan estate. Everyone else in the hovel got up and left very quickly. Neith glanced around, hushed him, and gathered them close: if there were not spies everywhere, there were certainly people willing to inform for a little ease. When Pterrence went on about the big secret at the estate, she grabbed him by the beak. "How do you know about the Padshah's secret?"

Anselem said they had heard it from a mushroom lady in a cave; what mattered was that they needed her help to find it.

Neith told them what she knew. Did they remember the grand hall where they had been raised up as the Padshah's own? Beyond it lay the family's private residence. Near the place where the manor connected to the entablature, the raised wing where the household's servants had lived and which had since collapsed, there was a room hidden behind a false wall. It led to a place the Padshah went seldom, and always alone. Neith had never been inside and did not know whether any of it still stood. The servants had told stories: that a djinn was housed there and granted the Padshah wishes; that the family performed dark rituals there to keep its power. But Neith had known the Padshah better than most, and loved her more, perhaps, than anyone ever had. She had been ruthless, cunning and ambitious, but she was not dark, not like the Templars. Whatever lay in that room, Neith believed, was solemn and sacred to the family.

They asked her to come with them; she did not deserve this labour. Neith was moved, but she could not. The labourers here worked in chain gangs, and if anyone was missing at the morning call, when the cultists delivered their sermons, the rest were made to suffer. But if they truly meant to free Korno, there was someone they needed to speak to: one of his lieutenants still led the cause. His name was [[Jalen Marek]]. The slums kept him hidden, and she could take them to him that very night.

## Jalen

Neith led them through slums as labyrinthine as they were squalid, half-buried ruins and broken streets funnelling them into alleys where even the desperate did not linger. At one corner she signalled to someone the party could not see, and a young voice spoke in their heads, directing them left down the alley and through an arch. There waited [[Grainseed|Ammat Grainseed]], the elven boy who had reached out to them telepathically when they first came to the slums to meet Korno, looking just as they remembered: pointed ears, smudged cheeks, clothes discoloured as much by age as by dirt. "Follow," his voice boomed in their minds. He led them through a courtyard of heaped rubble, down into broken earth hidden in a fold of refuse, along a narrow corridor reeking of sweat and smoke, and pulled aside a crude stone panel. "Safe house."

On the way in, Pterrence leaned over to ask Anselem whether he was allowed to talk about everything. Anselem shared his wisdom: when people look at you as if you are crazy, you stop saying it. The trick is that you do not know whether you should say something until you have said it. And when in doubt, keep your trap shut. Pterrence said he had never done that. Anselem said he knew, and had been meaning to tell him for a very long time.

The safe house was little more than a cavern carved out of the ruins, lit by guttering oil lamps and heavy with the smell of dried blood, fire and ash. Spears tipped with chipped obsidian, battered shields and scavenged swords leaned against the walls. Half a dozen men and women, weathered by hunger and the lash, watched in silence. Their leader rose and crossed to them with slow, measured steps: lean, scarred, sharp-eyed, carrying an exhaustion that weighed on his shoulders. This was Jalen Marek, the last of Korno's captains.

"So," he said, his voice hoarse from too much dust and too little water. "Neith speaks for you. That makes you better than most, but not by much."

Kit-Chac explained that they had told Neith they meant to rescue Korno, and she had said they must speak to him first. Jalen had seen what Raam had become: broken and chained, and Korno strung on the wall like a beast for the Templars to jeer at. He had thought his people were all that was left, and it was a comfort to meet friends. His heart said to free Korno, but his mind knew they might not be able to. If they could not break him loose, he said, they should end him. Korno would not want to be dragged into the Templars' games and made a puppet of the cult or of Jahandar.

Tchk'Tchk asked whether he had heard that Korno appeared refreshed and healed every day. Jalen had seen it with his own eyes, lingering as long as he dared. Pterrence told him they had heard Korno was to be executed on a holy day, Restoration Day, though no one yet knew when. Of course not, said Jalen: they had yet to hear from the Voice of Dregoth. Every morning and night the cultists preached that it was the duty of every living soul in Raam, by which they meant the slums, since he saw few nobles or merchants toiling, to rebuild the city in preparation for the Voice of Dregoth to speak to it as a sign that he had accepted them. Then the city would be restored on a holy day, sanctified by offering Korno to this god. As far as Jalen was concerned, they were all waiting for the cult to decide it had happened.

None of the party had heard of the Voice of Dregoth. All they could recall was the Sermon of the Crucible Truth, and the way it had been delivered: three cultists in dark robes and ceramic masks rising above the boulevard before the gates of the Templar district, speaking in a voice that seemed projected many times over. Perhaps that was the Voice. They did not know.

Anselem took the lead. In the Templars' eyes, he said, people like them were nothing but grist for the mill, and always would be; all they could do was spit in their eye by taking Korno from under their very noses. Whatever Jalen could tell them, they were ready to do what they could to bring Korno back to him. Even with the party's numbers, Jalen said, they were too few. But he leaned towards Anselem, eyes hard as obsidian: his people would stand with them, whether to free Korno or to cut him down. They had to choose their path and keep to it. There was no room left for half measures. Anselem told him they would take it as far as it needed to go, and that as for numbers, each of them was worth about five of Jalen's. Tchk'Tchk pointed out, not to brag, that they had just destroyed an entire colony of psychic spider monsters.

The plan, the party explained, was to get into the Templar district by night with forged papers, find Korno where he was held, and get him out. Jalen advised seeing the Nightrunners to make sure their papers would work. His own people would stage a distraction outside the Templar walls when the moment came, drawing off as many warriors and guards as they could so the party had time to search. And if the walls closed in with no way out, Jalen said, they must not let Korno keep breathing just to be made an offering to their false gods. Anselem urged him to make the distraction as chaotic as possible: the more chaos, the better the chance of slipping away into the crowd.

When did they plan to do it? Pterrence began listing everything else on their plate, starting with the papers and a big secret, until Anselem stopped him and Derfel revoked his decision-making privileges. "Tomorrow night, midnight," Anselem said. Jalen's people would be ready from midnight tomorrow and would wait for word; Neith and Ammat would know how to reach them, and they could be in position within the hour.

## Where Things Stand

The party has met [[Master Avariah]] and agreed to search the ruins of the [[Gulbadan Estate]] for the secret House Gulbadan kept about Raam's water. They still mean to free Korno, and to try the vaults beneath the Ivory Palace while they are in the Templar district, doing it all in one push before the alarm is raised. First they must get fresh work papers from the Nightrunners in [[The Elven Market]]. Jalen Marek's fighters stand ready to create a diversion. Salt has stayed below with Avariah, and the Hidden Spring cell is preparing to move.
