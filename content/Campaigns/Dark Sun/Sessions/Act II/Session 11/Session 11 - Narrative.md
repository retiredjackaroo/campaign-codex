## Out of the Hive

[[The Hive Mother]] was dead, and everything she had held together was coming apart. The black resin ribs that reinforced [[The Colony]] had been bound to her mind as surely as her children were. With the link severed, the structures flickered with a sickly green light and began to crack, and the Neogi themselves collapsed and convulsed, their minds shattering. A taskmaster clawed at its own head, shrieking inside every mind nearby. A [[Hook Horror]] stumbled blind, smashing into the walls.

![[Session 11 - The Hive Collapses.png]]
_The colony comes apart after Mother's death._

The party gathered at the mouth of Mother's chamber. [[Anselem]] had returned, [[Kit-Chac]] was with them again, and [[Salt]] had worked free of the rubble. [[Babs]], [[Pterrence]] and [[Tchk Tchk Gulbadani|Tchk'Tchk Gulbadani]] were unconscious. The only way out the party knew was the vent, three hard dashes away across a lair that was falling in on itself. Everyone who had carried hive strain still felt it, though no longer as a chorus: it had settled into a dull exhaustion that dragged at every effort. Only [[Derfel]] was clear of it.

They split the burden. Anselem took Babs. Kit-Chac took Tchk'Tchk. And, to everyone's surprise, Derfel bent down and scooped Pterrence into his arms. Nobody could tell whether his grimace came from the effort or from the fact that it was Pterrence.

## The First Stretch

Derfel ran first, forcing his way through the chamber entrance and down the tunnel into the wider corridors. Salt sprinted ahead to take point, flinging telekinetic force at falling stone and pushing rockslides aside just long enough for the others to pass. Anselem saw what Salt was doing and followed his path stone by stone, hopping between the safe spots in what looked like an ill-timed but effective game of hopscotch. Kit-Chac locked his lower arms around Tchk'Tchk's thorax, chitin against chitin, and used his upper arms to steady himself and shove rubble away as he parkoured over falling rock. The party covered the full distance.

As they burst into the corridor, Derfel and Kit-Chac both caught something beneath the noise of shattering stone and wailing minds: a faint, persistent hiss somewhere behind them, like gas escaping under pressure through a very small hole. Once heard, it could not be unheard. Neither could tell where it came from.

## The Floor Gives Way

The second tremor was worse. The floor buckled, great plates of stone tilting and sliding into the dark, and a fissure tore across the passage ahead. Behind them, the remaining Neogi writhed on the ground, isolated for the first time in their lives; one hook horror slammed itself against a wall again and again, while the other staggered after them screaming in a voice that was not quite its own. A boulder came down between the party and Mother's chamber. The way back was gone.

Derfel trusted the instincts of a lifetime crossing the wastes and juked through the hazards faster than a man carrying a Pterran should manage. Salt read the stone ahead, judging which passages would hold, and took the party down the safest of them. Anselem, flagging under Babs's weight, fixed his eyes on Salt and tried to anticipate his moves; when Salt took a turn he did not expect, Anselem ran straight into a collapsing wall. He kept hold of Babs, and later claimed he had swung her round to cushion the blow. Kit-Chac set Tchk'Tchk down, picked him up again by the neck plates in his mandibles like a lion with a cub, and bolted on all six remaining limbs. The floor split between his strides and dropped him flat. Even so, the party reached the wide corridor that led straight to the vent.

## The Last Stretch

A grinding roar rose from the rock itself. The walls bowed inward, resin ribs snapping one after another, and the dust grew so thick the runners could barely see each other. Someone screamed behind them, and the sound was swallowed by a tremendous crash. A last glimpse back showed black resin, stone and writhing limbs vanishing under rubble.

Derfel's instincts finally failed him. Blinded by dust, he turned straight into a rock wall that had not been there a moment before and became part of the rockslide. He tried to use Pterrence as a shield, without success. Pterrence's staff, tucked under his arm, jolted loose and fell. Salt spent psionic energy to stretch his limbs long, outpaced everyone who was carrying someone, and kept running rather than stop for the staff. Anselem put his head down and simply forced his way through. Kit-Chac rolled with his fall, slung Tchk'Tchk onto his back, and skittered low along the ground like a sand beetle on six limbs, picking the cleanest path across.

![[Session 11 - Anselem Carries Babs.png]]
_Anselem carries Babs through the collapsing tunnels._

## The Chasm

The vent was less than twenty feet away, but the ground in front of it had fallen into a gaping chasm. Behind them, the boulders were growing larger, sealing off corridors one by one. Then, somewhere back in the colony, came a great roiling roar and a crash, as if a massive wave of force had been driven at speed through a tight space before it broke.

Derfel scrambled across on instinct alone. Salt telekinetically set falling rocks beneath his feet as stepping stones; the first attempt went wrong, but he gathered himself, and as a boulder slammed down behind him the tilting ground seesawed up and threw him across the gap in a long, crouching leap. Anselem picked out the stable footholds, leapt to a ledge halfway across and made the rest from there. Kit-Chac folded Tchk'Tchk tight against his thorax with his lower arms, kept his upper arms free for the far ledge, and made the leap the way only a Thri-Kreen can.

One after another they scrambled up through the vent, pulling their unconscious friends after them, and dropped into the old slave pen where they had fought the Umber Hulks. It sat outside the heart of the colony. Dust poured through the vent and the tremors still shook the rock, but this chamber held.

## Grave

Derfel dropped Pterrence unceremoniously on the floor. Nobody had a healer's kit, and Salt had no spells left. Anselem, who at least had medical training, scraped some oddly coloured minerals and lichen off the walls, melted them down with his own heat into something between a poultice and a cautery, and worked it into his friends' wounds. It might have killed them. Instead Babs, Tchk'Tchk and Pterrence all came round.

Pterrence's first question was whether they had won. His second was why he had a lump on his head. His third, soon after, was where his staff was. It was somewhere under the colony now. Going back for it, everyone agreed, would be suicide; Pterrence decided they had made it out with their lives, and that was what mattered.

Then Pterrence noticed the dog. Derfel had called a new companion to his side: [[Grave]], his fourth. Pterrence did not remember the ceiling coming down on [[Brave]], and Derfel told him. "These poor little doggies," Pterrence said. The gods, the party agreed, seemed to have it in for their dogs.

## Back to the Spring

The party linked up with [[Tarek Cricket Silvertwig|Tarek]], [[Koresh Ironblood|Koresh]] and the prisoners they had freed, including the two Alliance scouts [[Narek]] and [[Scylla]]. The captives still did not speak, but now they turned their heads to meet the party as they came in: a first sign of awareness. With the Hive Mother dead and her colony buried, Tarek and Koresh were satisfied that the threat to [[The Hidden Spring]] was at least contained.

Before leaving, Pterrence cast Detect Magic as a ritual and walked them back through the slave pens, where piles of salvaged gear and personal effects lay among the rib-like cages. The two auras he found belonged to a bone dagger etched with runes and a spellbook, both of which the party had already recovered. They rested briefly, then spent the next couple of days travelling back through the dark to the sanctuary, recovering their strength along the way.

## News from Above

![[Session 11 - News from Above.png]]

The party returned to [[The Hidden Spring]] as heroes. Where the cell had once kept a polite distance, with Tarek the lone exception, they now made room for the party at meals and treated them almost as members of [[The Alliance]] themselves. [[Sarath]] admitted that when the party first arrived there had been a fierce debate about what to do with them. Knowing the way in, they could not have been allowed simply to leave, and Koresh had argued that knowing the Alliance existed at all was reason enough that they should not leave breathing. That had changed. She offered them an open invitation to join the cell, and promised that if they chose to go, no one would stop them. Even Koresh, scarred by the arena and haunted by his night terrors, sometimes smiled in their company now.

[[Leviath]] came and went through those days, and each evening she returned with grimmer news from [[Raam]]. [[The Templars]]' grip on the city was absolute. The Ivory Palace was their bastion, and [[High Kuatogagh Jahandar Shah|Jahandar Shah]] now ruled openly. The Nightrunners' knives still haunted [[The Elven Market]], but their leaders had slipped away, some said beyond the city walls. House M'Ke had become a licensed merchant house, governing trade and caravans in return for oaths of fealty to Jahandar. The nobles had been reduced to privileged subjects sworn to Templar law, and the survivors of [[The Slums]] had been enslaved or conscripted into labour camps, joined by anyone else who refused to bow. Pockets of resistance still fought on here and there.

Binding it all together was a new state religion. The masked clerics of Dregoth, in their heavy black robes and ceramic masks painted bronze, walked openly among the people, untouchable and revered. People spoke of miracles: resurrections, cleansed wells, the healing of blight. Their followers grew with every passing day.

## The Sermon of the Crucible Truth

![[Session 11 - Sermon of the Crucible Truth.png]]
_A masked cleric of Dregoth._

The party remembered one of the sermons the [[Dregoth Cultists|clerics of Dregoth]] preached. "All things struggle, all things wither, and all things serve," it began. Athas made people suffer not as punishment but as purpose; the sun scoured them in refinement, not hatred. "Suffering is the law of flesh." Beyond it lay "a crucible beyond death": in the name of Dregoth, first of the reborn, those who bowed, struggled without complaint and served without question would rise again, "unbroken, unburned, and unyielding," in a kingdom where the sun could not burn and the body could not fail. "We do not flee pain, we honor it. We do not resist order, we endure it. We do not beg the elements, we become them. So kneel in dust, bleed for now, and rise forever."

It was the creed of the Dragon Kings taken to its furthest extreme: endure, obey, and paradise will come later.

## Korno Lives

On her most recent return, Leviath brought news that struck at the heart. "[[Korno]] lives," she told them over supper. The Templars paraded him daily before the walls of the Templar district as a trophy, chained above one of its gates where the people could watch him caged and mocked. A holy day had been declared, the first festival dedicated to Dregoth, and on that day Korno and others held in the Templar district would be sacrificed as the city's first offering to its new god. Nobody yet knew when it would be. Leviath's face hardened. She had not always agreed with the man, she said, but she would not wish this fate on anyone, least of all him. "We must try to free him."

Pterrence agreed at once. Derfel pushed back. They had joined up to fulfil a contract for [[The Padshah]], and that contract was done; this sounded like suicide. Pterrence pointed out that they meant to break into the Templars' vault anyway. Kit-Chac said the clutch would decide together, and that Korno, though not clutch, was part of the pack, and the pack helps its own. Anselem was simple about it: "This is what we do. We undertake the missions that others cannot." Derfel looked to Babs, then gave in. "I don't like anyone upstairs, so fuck it, I'm in."

Sarath spoke up then, carefully. The fourth of the Alliance's aims was to oppose defilers, and these clerics surely qualified. But Korno was not of the Alliance, and acting in his name risked revealing the cell to the Templars and endangering every other cell besides. She would not stop the party, but she judged the decision beyond her authority. She asked for one more evening while [[Nisat of the Cavern Moss|Nisat]] sent word to [[Master Avariah]]. Nisat went to the grotto pool, plucked a shimmering green-and-blue dragonfly from among the mushrooms and flowers, whispered into his cupped hands, and released it through the illusory wall.

Sarath then asked whether the party would go even without the Alliance's aid. Pterrence said of course they would; Kit-Chac agreed. Derfel glowered in the background at the idea of walking into the unknown with no scouting, no preparation and no plan.

## A Plan Takes Shape

They talked it through with Leviath and Tarek. Korno was brought out each morning and chained to the wall above the main gate onto the Templar district, on a busy thoroughfare between the Elven Market and the trade districts, then taken back inside each evening. The gates stood open by day, guarded by at least half a dozen Templars and a few priests of Dregoth, with more close enough to respond. The party considered sending Korno word through psionic or magical means, but agreed the Templars would hardly leave that avenue unguarded: a message might be detected, blocked, or even answered by someone else. The festival was no secret, Leviath said, but the city was still rebuilding after the Inundation, and she thought there was time.

The party still held the forged work orders [[Chazhal|the Pitch]] had given them for the Ivory Palace, though they were weeks old now and might no longer pass. The Nightrunners kept a presence in the Elven Market even with their leaders gone, so Pitch might yet be found.

The shape of a plan emerged: a small distraction to clear civilians from the thoroughfare, then a larger blast, perhaps a cart left near the gate, to draw the Templars in; Pterrence's Fog Cloud over the gate; and a quick grab for Korno in the confusion. Tarek grinned at the idea of explosions. Leviath, who did not share his ease with harm to innocents, insisted that as many civilians as possible be clear before anything big went off.

Tarek was bound by the aims, but he admitted he was tired of sitting in the sanctuary; he could not remember the last time the cell had actually subverted a defiler. He offered to procure the makings of something that went boom from the cell's craftsmen and from Nisat's knowledge of volatile foraged materials, though he would have to be a little deceptive about why, since Nisat followed the aims as strictly as Sarath did. Leviath was not bound by the aims, but she was known to the Templars and marked as an enemy, so her direct involvement would add risk. If the mission went ahead, the cell would abandon this safe house, leaving signs that would lead the party to wherever it relocated.

## Downtime

While they waited, Pterrence found a replacement weapon among the cell's equipment and carved himself a new staff tipped with obsidian. He also began carving a bow for Derfel: a +1 longbow that would take at least ten days of work and two hundred ceramic pieces. Derfel paid, then put both hands on Pterrence's shoulders, leaned in until they were almost forehead to forehead, and said: "Don't fuck it up."

[[The Voices Amulet|The Voice's Amulet]] was finally identified. It bore the insignia of the Templar order that oversaw Raam's tanneries and dyeing industries, and when attuned it acted as an amulet of the devout, sharpening its wearer's spells and granting a free use of Channel Divinity each day. Anselem took it. The bone dagger Tchk'Tchk had been using proved to be a +1 blade that lets its attuned wielder parry several times a day. Kit-Chac offered to cover the cost of Pterrence learning new spells from his spellbook, and Pterrence planned to take him up on it once he advanced. Kit-Chac also drew a rough map of the gate where Korno was displayed, from Leviath's descriptions and his own memory of the area. Pterrence handed [[The Skull|the elemental skull fragment]] to the cell; they had already placed it under containment, since a single command word would let it reform into its elemental.

## The Summons

The following evening, as the cell gathered by the grotto pool for their meagre meal, the dragonfly returned. It circled the cavern once, settled at the water's edge with its body glowing like jade under lamplight, and parted its wings. A woman's voice filled the grotto, not from the insect but through the stone and water themselves, measured and solemn, with heartache beneath its poise:

"Leviath, you will bring them to me. The strangers who walk beside you are to be known to me in full. Make no delay. The place will be prepared."

Then the voice ended, and the dragonfly spiralled up through the illusory veil. Sarath bowed her head, Nisat murmured a prayer, and Koresh's jaw tightened. To the cell, Avariah's word was not guidance but law. Tarek spat into the dust: she had a way of listening while having already decided, he said, as if she played a game on a board only she could see. He would follow, they all would. Sarath shot him a sharp look, and he raised his hands in mock surrender.

Kit-Chac wondered aloud why Avariah would want to meet them rather than simply answer yes or no. Derfel thought it smelled like a trap, but Leviath had brought them to safety and they owed her a debt, which was more than they could say of Korno. Leviath told them not to mistake the message for dismissal: there was no order to risk lives and no decree, only a request to meet. The place was not far, and she would take them.

Around them, the cell's own unease showed. Tarek joked that they used to break staffs over defilers' skulls and now they mapped fungus patches in the deep dark. Koresh said the Master believed the Underdark hid what they needed, though he would rather face a defiler in daylight than crawl through stone chasing shadows. Nisat said the aims were not always a straight path, and that Avariah saw something in the deep places that tied back to the war above. Sarath said she had seen Avariah's judgement tested before and never found it wanting. Whatever the reason, the cell's focus had shifted from striking at defilers to charting tunnels.

The party agreed to go and meet the Master.
